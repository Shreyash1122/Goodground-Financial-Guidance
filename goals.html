Choose what matters now

One step at a time.

These are common family priorities, not a checklist. Start wherever it feels most useful for your household.

01 / STEADY GROUND
✳

Build a buffer for the unexpected

An accessible emergency fund can help your household manage surprise expenses or income changes.

  • List a few costs that would be hardest to cover unexpectedly.
  • Choose a starter amount that feels achievable.
  • Keep emergency savings accessible and separate from day-to-day spending.
02 / A PLACE TO CALL HOME
⌂

Make a home goal clearer

Look at the full monthly cost of owning a home, including loan payments, upkeep and other regular expenses.

  • Estimate a down payment and associated purchase costs.
  • Compare a potential EMI with your other commitments.
  • Leave space for repairs and changing expenses.
03 / ROOM TO GROW
✿

Prepare for education costs

Education plans can span many years. A rough timeline and periodic check-ins help make the goal easier to revisit.

  • Write down the timing and likely costs you can estimate today.
  • Review your plan as school and family needs change.
  • Keep education savings balanced with other household needs.
04 / FUTURE YOU
↗

Give retirement a place in the plan

Starting early can give savings more time, but the right approach depends on your needs, timeline and comfort with risk.

  • Note the age or life stage you’re planning around.
  • Understand the risks, costs and tax rules of any product.
  • Seek qualified advice before making investment decisions.
05 / MORE ROOM EACH MONTH
◉

Make debt feel more manageable

A clear list of balances, interest rates and due dates can help you have a more informed repayment conversation.

  • Gather current statements and minimum payments.
  • Contact your lender if a payment is becoming difficult.
  • Avoid taking on new debt to cover recurring shortfalls where possible.
06 / PROTECT WHAT MATTERS
♡

Review your family’s protection

Insurance needs vary by family. Understand what a policy covers, its exclusions, costs and claim process.

  • Check existing coverage and important exclusions.
  • Compare the total cost and benefit terms.
  • Talk with a qualified advisor about your household’s needs.
