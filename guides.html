Useful questions for your next step.

These guides are general education. The right answer depends on your family’s circumstances and current rules.

01 / MONTHLY MONEY

How do we start a family budget?

Begin with income you can count on, regular essentials and debt payments. Then account for less frequent costs before deciding what can go toward goals.

A simple first conversation

What bills are fixed? Which costs change month to month? What annual expenses surprised us last year? Which goal matters most for the next few months? Keep the first pass approximate and revisit it after a month.

02 / EMERGENCIES

What is an emergency fund for?

It’s savings set aside for unplanned costs or disruptions, so the household may have more options when something unexpected happens.

Where can we begin?

Identify one or two realistic surprise expenses and choose a first savings milestone. Consider accessibility, safety and any applicable account terms. Your ideal amount depends on income stability, dependents and commitments.

03 / BORROWING

What should we understand about an EMI?

A monthly payment is only one part of a loan. The rate, term, fees, penalties and total amount repaid all affect the cost.

Questions to ask a lender

Is the rate fixed or variable? What fees apply? How can the rate or payment change? Are there prepayment charges? What happens if a payment is late? Read the current loan documents and ask the lender to clarify terms before signing.

04 / SAVING & INVESTING

How do we think about a long-term goal?

Write down the goal, approximate timing and how much uncertainty you can handle. Different savings and investment options have different risks and terms.

Before choosing a product

Understand possible loss, liquidity, fees, tax treatment and whether returns are guaranteed. Verify providers and product details through official sources, and consider an appropriately qualified financial professional.

05 / FAMILY PLANNING

How can we make money talks easier?

Pick a calm time, agree on one topic and keep the conversation focused on shared priorities rather than blame.

Try a 20-minute check-in

What went well this month? What felt tight? Is there a bill or goal coming up? What is one small action we can take together? Write down the action and a date to review it.

06 / GETTING HELP

When should we talk to a professional?

Consider qualified help when a decision is complex, involves significant money or has legal, tax, insurance or investment consequences.

How to prepare

Gather current statements, list your questions and ask about qualifications, fees, conflicts of interest and the scope of advice. Never share passwords, OTPs or account access codes.
